# Fire Emblem: Three Houses + Expansion Pass 

Three Houses is a very fun game at its core, let down by surrounding mechanics and scope.

I originally started this game on Maddening, but as this was my first Fire Emblem the difficulty was pretty ridiculous. After I somehow managed to pass the initial mock battle, I dropped the difficulty to Hard, and Hard was a much more manageable difficulty... until it became too manageable. Despite being called Hard it feels a lot more like a "Normal" and after the first few chapters the amount of thinking you need to do significantly lowers. And well this game made the silly decision to block you off of increasing the difficulty (instead only letting you drop it), so if things are starting to ease up on you, you need to start finding your own ways to try to add a challenge or just accept the difficulty drop. I don't mind an easy game by any means if they still provide things to make it interesting to play, but there were points throughout the game where things were approaching drain-dead territory because well... this game has a lot of repetitive battles.
Namely the Auxiliary battles. While the story battles give you unique maps and enemies and gimmicks to fight against and are generally very enjoyable (even if again not *that* hard), after the first few chapters not only do the Auxiliary battles become very easy, but they also constantly repeat the same few maps. They do randomise the enemies on the map...? As if that matters. You could make the argument to just ignore the Auxiliary Battles, and if you're on some challenge run that would make sense, but they are also by far the best way to gain experience and resources, so on a casual playthrough this seems completely counter-intuitive. Honestly there being so few maps honestly just made Fodlan seem tiny, which is really sad.
It is genuinely a shame as well, because on the whole this battle system is really fun to play with. This was my first SRPG and I loved all the mechanics I had to play with, it was just a shame how little I had to use a lot of them (because again most battles were just stupid Auxiliary ones and the difficulty was on the easier side).
Onto something a bit lighter, exploring the Monastery is... okay. The Monastery does have its good parts like fishing, gardening and dining with your students, stuff like that. But really there just isn't enough things to do in it, and some of the things you can do (The quests and lost items) are incredibly dull. For lost items specifically it was really funny when I beat the DLC and that unlocked the ability of getting a hint on who the lost items belonged to. Essentially the developers admitting the lost items sucked without saying anything outright. 
Luckily the Monastery does build into what is probably the best part of the game (outside of the good battles), and that is unit building! I really enjoyed the class system and teaching all my units moves. It took a little bit to understand at first but once it clicked it was very engaging and I looked forward to the lecture sessions whenever they were upcoming.
Unfortunately the unit building is let down a little bit by the UI. While the UI looks decent visually, things feel like they're all over the place and it's a pain to trudge through it all. You do eventually learn where everything is, but even then the UI remains fiddly for the entire game.
Graphics wise this game looks like a 3DS game that they were told last minute had to go onto Switch. A such it's poorly optimised performance wise and while it does have a nice art direction in theory, the execution is a bit shoddy. I will say the character models and artwork for this game are extremely well made, and it's mostly the environments that are really quite poor. But either way, it didn't really bother me much and visually it's fine overall.
Speaking of characters, they are another highlight of this game! There is somehow so many characters and yet all of them get quite a bit of time to be fleshed out. Some of them do have kinda silly gimmicks, but most of them are very interesting. The support cutscenes are really nice and I'm shocked at how many there are.
As for the overall story, it's also fine. The first half of the game was nice and I was genuinely interested in the story. My playthrough did the Silver Snow route however, and unfortunately most of the Silver Snow content is very lacklustre making me kinda drop off the story until the end. I'm sure the other routes have it much better than what I got, but the story isn't too crazy from everything I know. I'm not disappointed by it - it had some good moments throughout - but there wasn't really enough for me to really like it or anything.
On a slightly more upbeat note though, the music in this game is sooooo good. Like wow it really helped keep me at least slightly engaged during the 10th repeat of a map in an Auxiliary battle. Thank you composers and performers!

FE:3H is a game that is not awful in any metric and has many fun moments throughout, but is unfortunately dragged down by just trying to be bigger than it is. I suppose my top 3 changes (in no particular order) would be:

* Monastery was shrunken down to reflect how much you actually do in it
* Auxiliary battles got reworked to be much less tedious
* The difficulty got readjusted

But as is, the game is still a decently fun time and I enjoyed it overall!
