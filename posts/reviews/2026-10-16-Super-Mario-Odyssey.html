Super Mario Odyssey

Posted on: 2026-10-16

Super Mario Odyssey is a game brimming with life. It has a fun premise, amazing visuals and sound design, and throughout the adventure there are so many interesting and cool moments that have so much pure joy and whimsy. This game has probably my favourite Mario final boss, the main story is short but sweet, and it has a post-game brimming with content. While I enjoyed my time overall with this game and it's superbly well made and polished, it is let down slightly by 2 quite important elements, which I will discuss below.

Mario's Movement:

Mario is very fun and fluid to control and mess around with but in practice when playing the game he can often be disappointing.

Turning around at a stand-still is surprisingly Mario 64-like (it's finicky unless you do a 180). When Mario is going at all fast and starts moving over something that isn't flat ground he quickly becomes hard to control. And while you don't really notice this in normal gameplay since you are doing anything but this, the acceleration curve on the ground is awkward and it makes the Volleyball mini-game... kinda awful.

Now Mario has a very unbalanced move-set and it really comes down to 1 move. The Cappy-throw-dive is very easy to pull off for how much it can skip things throughout the game. You do it so often you wish it was 1 button because the novelty of the button combo eventually wears off. That being said it should've actually been made harder or significantly nerfed because all the level design is accounting for you not knowing it... even though it's relatively simple?

Now whether you Cappy-throw-dive or just move around the kingdoms in general you will definitely do this at least once: Bonking. It is immensely unfun. In previous games there were far less opportunities to bonk since less moves left you open to it. Mario 64 even lets you save yourself from it if it was from a long jump, making sure that if you do bonk, it's rare and a fair enough punishment for poor movement. In SMO, your most versatile movement options in this game are rolling (bonkable) and diving (bonkable) so it happens all the time. Bonking also has far too long of a recovery here, not doing it any favours, and it should've been a lot more lenient because so often you'll barely be touching a wall or not even going that fast but you still get punished for it.

I also have an issue with the motion controls. Cappy is very easy to accidentally set off because he can be triggered by a shake input? The Cappy spin throw gave me genuine wrist pain on my right wrist, needing me to take a break and then relearn it with my opposite hand. The Cappy down throw also literally never works. Constantly shaking to roll is annoying. A bunch of the captures have tacked on motion control gimmicks that either don't feel natural or are just far more annoying than a button press. I will concede that when I tried split joy-cons for a bit it did feel a bit more comfortable (like the game recommends), however I was mostly playing on pro controller or in handheld mode which are still valid options, and it doesn't change the fact that I think that most of the motion controls here are poorly thought out. Super Mario Galaxy back on the Wii was far better than this...

Because rolling and diving is mapped to the same input as Cappy there were many cases where I did a move I did not want to because you can't always differentiate between them in play. If you want to then mix the 2 types of moves together, you'd need to use the other action button... except it's mapped all the way up at X. This means that for example, I can't do stuff like rolling into Cappy at all comfortably. I had to resort to remapping the other action button to ZR in the Switches Settings, so I could at least do something about it, but I feel bad for people playing the game around its launch, and they really should've thought it through a bit more.

Look, Mario is fun to control here overall, and there are some genuinely cool things you can do thanks to his expressive movement throughout the game, but I was feeling the above negative points nagging at me in the back of my head constantly throughout the game because they really do come up often even if they are all minor annoyances individually.

Moons & Exploration:

There are a lot of completely pointless moons that make moons overall feel worthless. You start feeling less accomplished when you get a hard moon cause you just picked up 3 easy ones. It's kinda messed up when some of the purple coins can feel harder than actual moons. That being said it does give the game a fast feeling pacing, and at least you feel rewarded for almost anywhere you decide to look at, for better or worse? While these thoughts start weighing on you the more you play the game, I luckily think that this game doesn't drag out long enough that I started to really hate exploring and collecting.

It is important to note that I went through the main story quite quickly, with the knowledge that this game adds a bunch of extra moons to each kingdom once completed. This saved me from exploring every kingdom twice instead of 1 big sweep through. If I did end up looking through every kingdom twice, or if I decided to go for 100%, or if I ever replay the game in the future, I have a feeling the novelty of all the moons really would fade on me.

So those are my 2 gripes with this game. I didn't spend too much time talking about the good parts since those have been talked about to death, but I still really liked it and would recommend playing it. It's just definitely not my favourite 3D Mario, that's for sure.
